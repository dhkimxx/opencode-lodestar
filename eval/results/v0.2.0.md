# Evaluation results for v0.2.0

Measured on 2026-10-20 with OpenCode 2.0.27 and the harness in [`eval/`](../README.md).

## Setup

- **Tasks.** 40 tasks from SWE-bench Pro V2, four from each of ten repositories: NodeBB, ansible, element-web, flipt, vuls, teleport, openlibrary, navidrome, webclients, and qutebrowser. They are in Go, Python, JavaScript, and TypeScript.
  - Three per repository are large tasks: the ones whose reference patch size is closest to that repository's 95th percentile, among the tasks that touch at least four files.
  - The fourth is the repository's largest task from HARD-51, a subset most model families fail.
  - None of them are in `eval/tasks.json`. `eval/tasks-hard.json` lists them, and `eval/README.md` says how they were picked.
- **Model.** `opencode-go/deepseek-v4.1-flash` through OpenCode Go, with reasoning effort `high`.
- **Configurations.** A is OpenCode alone. B is OpenCode with lodestar. Every task ran once per configuration: 80 runs, or 40 pairs of A and B on the same task. All 80 counted.
- **Protocol.** Each run follows the benchmark's own rules. The agent works inside the task's container, with the network closed except for the model API, and gets 50 minutes. Its diff is then graded in a fresh container by the task's hidden tests.
- **Compaction.** Automatic compaction starts at 40,000 tokens (`--threshold 40000`). This makes compaction happen often. In 29 of the 40 pairs, both runs compacted, many of them several times.

## Where the difference came from

- **The hardest tasks.** On the 10 HARD-51 tasks, A resolved 6 and B resolved 9. On the 30 large tasks, both resolved 28.
- **Repeated compaction.** At 40,000 tokens, a run whose context fills up quickly compacts again within a few steps. Three of the four tasks resolved only with lodestar were of this kind:
  - In those three, A ran out its 50 minutes after 34 to 42 compactions.
  - B finished the same tasks in 14 to 30 minutes, with 11 to 23 compactions.
  - In the fourth, both runs used their full 50 minutes.
- **Restored work replaced re-reading.** With the request, the latest steps, and the changed files restored, agents read 58% fewer files again after a compaction. They also made 39% fewer tool calls overall.
- **The one task resolved only without lodestar.** In webclients `8be4f6`, both runs took about 32 minutes. The lodestar run compacted 10 times to A's 7, then ended with a summary of work it considered finished.

## What else was checked

- **What lodestar restored.** 6.1k tokens per compaction on average.
- **The recap request doesn't end runs early.** B runs got 221 recap requests. Two runs ended right after one, and in both the reply was the agent's final summary of finished work.
- **Cache loss during compaction comes from OpenCode.**
  - OpenCode caps its compaction request at the auto-compaction threshold, and it estimates the request's size at four characters per token.
  - When that estimate doesn't fit, OpenCode rebuilds the history as plain text, which loses the prompt cache with or without lodestar.
  - At 40,000 tokens this happened to 46% of A's compactions and 43% of B's. That is why uncached tokens fell less than prompt tokens.
  - The harness's `--compact-at` avoids it, and later evaluations use it.

## Limitations

- **One model.** These results are for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash through OpenCode Go.
- **The resolve rate isn't settled.** Four tasks went to lodestar and one against it. With 40 pairs, that is within chance (exact McNemar test, p = 0.38).
- **Compaction is forced early.** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash has a 1M-token window, and real sessions compact later and less often. The low threshold also made OpenCode rebuild compaction requests as text, which raised uncached tokens in both configurations.
- **One run per task and configuration.** Single pairs are noisy. The pooled numbers above are what to rely on.
- **Time includes contention.** Three runs ran at once, so a run's test commands shared CPU with others, which adds noise to minutes.

The 80 runs cost $7.66 and took 17.5 hours of agent time.
